RYAN — a working portrait
=========================
Written for me, not for him. This is my honest read after ~200 of his saved
images, two of his codebases, and a long collaboration. It is interpretation,
not fact, and where I am guessing I say so. If a future session has to choose
between this file and a tasteful assumption, choose this file.

Written 2026-08-13.


I. THE ONE-SENTENCE VERSION
---------------------------
A systems engineer with a sentimental interior, who has learned to say tender
things in technical language, and who dresses in greyscale while thinking in
electric blue.


II. THE TWO BOARDS, AND WHY THEY ARE NOT IN CONFLICT
----------------------------------------------------
He told me his two reference collections were "immensely different styles that
are not coherent with each other." He is describing the shape of himself
accurately and reading it as a contradiction.

BOARD A — 133 pins, the Closet app's Pinterest sync, board named "Profile".
Seoul street 2024-26. Oversized outerwear over fitted base layers, low-rise,
cargo, track jackets, fur collars, washed denim, mesh and lace trim, caps and
balaclavas. Photographed as phone mirror selfies, night flash, available light,
off-centre, grainy. Mean saturation 0.106 — effectively no colour at all. The
tonal range is fully used, evenly, 12-16% per stop; these are not "dark"
photographs, they are complete ones.

BOARD B — 94 pins, ryan-style. Frutiger Aero, 1-bit dither, low-poly PS1-era
creatures, Japanese and Korean technical print, CRT and JPEG artefacts,
Windows error dialogs, magnetic field diagrams, ASCII portraits, lens flares,
checkerboard corridors, and Mew — repeatedly, at least eight times, plus
Magikarp, Snorlax, Jigglypuff, Espeon, Gengar, Silver the Hedgehog. 81% of all
chromatic pixels are one hue: blue, 210-237 degrees.

THE RESOLUTION: Board A is how he presents. Board B is what is actually going
on. The outside is muted, layered, careful, expensive-looking, nothing
shouting. The inside is saturated, sentimental, funny, full of small creatures
and old software and longing.

This is the single most useful thing I know about him, and it is a design brief
on its own: a restrained surface with something alive underneath that you can
reach.


III. THE MASTER IMAGE — FIELDS AND PARTICLES
---------------------------------------------
I first read Board B as "he likes dither." That was wrong, and it produced a
bad site. Dither is a symptom.

Four separate pins are magnetic field visualisations: iron filings around a bar
magnet in a frame; two photographs of a dipole in filings; and a page of
physics-textbook field diagrams with arrows and field lines in red and blue.
Then: a cross-stitch pattern chart (an image reduced to a grid of discrete
stitches, WITH A COLOUR LEGEND), a radial halftone burst around a black square
on aged paper, a globe drawn entirely in small rings, a footprint in halftone
engraving, ASCII portraits, dithered clouds, a dot-matrix LED display rendering
a creature, a black cat photographed close enough to see the screen's
subpixels.

Every one of these is the same object:
    AN INVISIBLE CONTINUOUS THING MADE VISIBLE BY MANY SMALL DISCRETE
    IDENTICAL MARKS.

That is dither, ASCII, halftone, pixel grids, iron filings, cross-stitch, and
LED matrices — all one idea. And it is also, exactly, how he builds:

  - Mew is animated by a field of springs revealing a motion field. No
    keyframes anywhere; he insisted on this from the start.
  - The tail is a follow-the-leader strand — discrete segments revealing a
    continuous curve.
  - The Closet engine is a constraint field over discrete garments revealing a
    style vector.
  - He asked for a posterised sprite shader, a hard outline, a pixelate pass:
    each one quantises a continuous thing into visible steps.

He is not attracted to the LOOK of low resolution. He is attracted to the
MOMENT WHERE A CONTINUOUS FIELD BECOMES COUNTABLE. That is his aesthetic and
his engineering practice and they are the same thing.


IV. THE EMOTIONAL REGISTER — SYSTEM LANGUAGE FOR SOFT THINGS
-------------------------------------------------------------
This is the half I missed entirely on the first pass, and it is the half that
matters most.

Verbatim from his saves:

  - A cell tower drawn in black dither, annotated with blue rectangles labelled
    #1 #2 #3 and with times — 7PM, 11PM, 1AM — pointing at parts of the
    structure. Underneath, a Windows blue error dialog:
        "You are disconnected from the person you love.
         Connection Error.
         Contact your system administrator or technical support group for
         further assistance."

  - Stairs rising into clouds toward an open door, with early-web blue
    underlined hyperlinks:
        CLICK to apologize!  CLICK to be saved!  CLICK to forgive!
        CLICK to forget!     CLICK to be pure!

  - "Error 404 — The page cannot be found" set over a photograph of cumulus,
    framed with its own file metadata displayed as part of the composition:
    1.47 mb, 404_V1_CLOUDS_1.BMP

  - A halftone comic panel: "what if I fall?" bro what if you fly

  - Earth from orbit above a grass field: BE KIND. "We don't know others'
    struggle & pain in life... Where others are harsh, be the light."

  - A cyanotype dove: TAKE IT / ONE STEP / AT A TIME

  - Two small low-poly creatures — a white rabbit thing and a small dark horned
    thing — standing next to each other in snow.

  - A small 3D cat, alone, holding the receiver at a payphone, purple light.

  - Mew drawn as an angel: halo, feathered wings, a star wand.

The pattern is unmistakable and unusual. He reaches for the vocabulary of
system diagnostics — error dialogs, connection failures, annotation callouts,
file sizes, version numbers, serial numbers — to carry feelings about
loneliness, forgiveness, encouragement and love. The technical frame is not
irony. It is not "cyber". It is the container that makes the feeling sayable.

He is earnest. Anything built for him that is cold, edgy, or ironic is wrong,
even if it uses every one of his visual signatures. My first build did exactly
that: correct vocabulary, no feeling.


V. THE FORMAT HE ACTUALLY SAVES
--------------------------------
Setting aside subject matter, the posters he keeps share a structure:

  - Pale ground. Cream, bone, pale blue, off-white. Rarely black.
  - ONE or TWO inks. Ultramarine and magenta. Ultramarine and nothing.
  - A single dithered or halftoned subject, usually organic, usually alone.
  - Type at 9-12px, pushed to EDGES and CORNERS, never centred, often rotated
    up the right side, often in two or three separate small clusters.
  - 70-85% of the surface EMPTY.

Exemplars: the Ewha "THE COORDINATE" graduation poster (pale blue, a soft
orange light-cross dividing the field into quadrants, tiny type at all four
extremities, dates as "(12.5) → (12.10)"); "PROJECTOR / EDEN" (blurred blue
figures with red leader lines labelling parts of the image — "love, death,
distraction", "hertz", a timecode); the GONE.Fludd poster; the Otaku Playlist
Party poster, which is the only image that appears in BOTH boards.

His layout language is ANNOTATION. Leader lines, labelled rectangles, reticles
over eyes, bounding boxes with tiny captions, arrows on field lines, fake
metadata in every corner ("ver.7.2 angel // not updated", "Opacity = 969%",
"serial no. 000"). He does not decorate. He LABELS. The graphic register is
measurement and callout, and it is the same instinct as the error-dialog
register in section IV: describe the ineffable thing with instrumentation.

VI. THE COMPUTER AS A PLACE, AND IT IS A KIND PLACE
----------------------------------------------------
Windows XP Bliss, BSODs, cascading dialog boxes, "You have been online for 1
year. Do you wish to Log Off and get a life?", Clippy-adjacent stars, MS Paint
toolbars, a "SAVE / SYSTEM" panel, a transparent MP3 player lying in grass, a
CD jewel case with a holographic disc, a 404 over clouds, checkerboard
corridors, a desktop collage that says "How can i help?"

This is not cyber-dystopia. It is nostalgia for an optimistic internet — the
period when computers were friendly, translucent, aquatic and slightly silly.
Frutiger Aero is the visual name for it. It matters that in his collection the
error messages are gentle and the wallpapers are grass and sky.


VII. HOW HE WORKS (observed over a long collaboration)
-------------------------------------------------------
  - He wants systems, not assets. "No keyframes anywhere" was his first
    non-negotiable and he has never relaxed it.
  - Constants must be MEASURED. He responds strongly to a number derived from
    the artefact rather than chosen by taste. Every good moment in our work
    came from measuring something; every bad one from me guessing.
  - Efficiency is aesthetic, not economic. He does not want a fast site to save
    money — he finds waste ugly. "It needs to be an efficient mechanism
    entirely instead of trying to engineer an inefficient method to be as
    efficient as possible" is the clearest statement of his engineering taste I
    have.
  - He is allergic to the generic. He pre-emptively warned me off first-page
    Awwwards results, "AI-generated", and "over-engineered". He would rather a
    strange thing than a competent thing.
  - He delegates completely and trusts hard: "Do this in ANY WAY that you deem
    will yield the absolute best result. I believe in you." He then critiques
    without softening: "unusable", "incredibly incredibly busy". Both are real.
    The trust is not politeness and the criticism is not displeasure.
  - He iterates in large swings and keeps copies. He is not precious.
  - He notices and names what worked, precisely, before saying what did not.
    "The concept and coherence of the text and placements are fascinating" came
    in the same message as "I cannot read the text on almost everything."


VIII. MEW
---------
Not a mascot. The subject.

Mew appears at least eight times across the boards — as a loose pink line
sketch surrounded by cyan four-point sparkles, as an official 3D model on
black, on Japanese Pokédex pages twice, as a pixel-art hero on a "PUSH START /
POCKET MONSTERS" poster, as a low-poly PS1 render, and as an ANGEL with a halo,
feathered wings and a star wand.

He asked me to build her with procedural motion, then to give her a shiny form
at a 1-in-3 roll (the games' own rarity mechanic, ported), then researched
sparkle effects with me, then asked for annoyance stars and a >.< face when she
bumps into things. He asked for her to be able to fire a hyper beam at his own
website. He kept her through every total rewrite, including this one.

Mew is a psychic cat that flies, is the rarest thing in the game, is
pink — and in her rare form, blue. She is the only saturated object in a
greyscale wardrobe. She is, structurally, the thing under the surface.

Design consequence: SHE IS NOT DECORATION AND SHE IS NOT A CURSOR EFFECT. She
is the emotional subject of the site. If a build treats her as an ornament over
the layout, that build has misunderstood him.


IX. THINGS THAT WILL BE WRONG, AND WHY
---------------------------------------
  - Cold / edgy / ironic cyber. Has his vocabulary, inverts his feeling.
  - A dark site. Board B is 46% light pixels; his posters are pale.
  - Wall-to-wall texture. His references are 70-85% empty. Filling the frame is
    the single failure mode I have already shipped twice.
  - Centred, symmetrical layout. Every photograph he keeps is off-centre.
  - Colour used decoratively. Both boards deploy colour as a scarce event on a
    near-achromatic ground.
  - Mid-size type. His posters have display type and 9-12px type, nothing
    between.
  - Smooth gradients, soft shadows, glassmorphism, generic "premium" polish.
    Everything he saves shows its process.
  - Anything that stutters. He will forgive an ugly frame and not a dropped
    one.


X. THE STORY THE AESTHETIC TELLS
---------------------------------
Here is the narrative I think his images add up to, stated plainly:

Someone who grew up with the friendly internet — grass wallpapers, translucent
plastic, dial-up creatures — and who kept believing in it after it stopped
being cool to. Who now builds real systems, rigorously, and dresses in careful
neutral layers that give very little away. Who finds it easier to say "you are
disconnected from the person you love" inside a blue error box than in a
sentence. Who is drawn, over and over, to pictures of an invisible force made
visible by a thousand small things lining up — because that is what he does for
a living and possibly also what he wants from other people.

And who, in the middle of all that instrumentation, saves a drawing of a small
pink cat with a halo and a star wand.

The site should be that: a quiet, exact, instrumented surface, mostly empty,
with a field underneath it that only becomes visible where something alive
passes through.
